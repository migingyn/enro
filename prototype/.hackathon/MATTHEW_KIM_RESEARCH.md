## What each project made demonstrable

- **Flow:** Speak a topic, enter a generated 3D learning space, and ask the narrator questions about the current view. Voice becomes part of exploration and teaching. The submission reports approximately five minutes for fresh world generation and a reusable scene library; do not interpret the concept-to-world pitch as instantaneous generation. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/flow-8pgm1k)
- **Dialed:** Observe an Instagram feed, identify manipulative content, and show an intervention. The visible browser and agent activity help explain the system. A submission update says the public site offers a recorded/demo run because of API costs, with live operation offered in person. Fetch.ai also awarded a **special mention**, explaining the portfolio’s second recognition without implying a second first-place prize. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/dialed), [sponsor announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/fetch-ai-innovation-lab_agentverse-fetchai-hackathon-activity-7441860073456422912-h8Dj)
- **Iris:** Select a subject or region, describe the change, and see it carried through video. A visual before/after communicates the value quickly; temporal consistency gives the technical work a concrete purpose. The overall placement is stronger evidence of broad judging success than a sponsor-specific badge, though no public scorecard explains its result. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/iris-b7poh2), [portfolio](https://mykm.dev/projects/iris)
- **Nami:** A college counseling office with specialist characters and answers tied to source documents. Human Delta supports the shared grounding layer, so its contribution can be demonstrated by following a claim back to evidence. The first-place award is specifically Human Delta’s category. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/advisr), [portfolio](https://mykm.dev/projects/nami)
- **Ione:** A tutor observes work, detects mistakes, and offers help with voice and persistent learning context. The student’s ongoing work supplies the input, reducing the need to formulate a separate prompt. This is an ElevenLabs win, not an independently verified overall or Education award. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/ione), [portfolio](https://mykm.dev/projects/ione)
- **Kali:** A nonprofit operations interface connects donor, grant, and finance questions with cited records and onchain fundraising. The submission explicitly describes fictional deterministic fixtures behind its SaaS connectors; production OAuth connections remain future work. Payment demonstrations use Solana devnet, with simulation available. The transferable lesson is coherent workflow coverage and a relevant sponsor use case, not that a weekend produced a deployed nonprofit integration platform. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/kali-40emhw)
- **Bro:** Conversation produces persistent memory, a growing graph, and an inspectable record of work. Voice, memory, and market context support three sponsor stories. Solana reads and prices feed paper trading; this does not establish real-money execution. Its writeup says unavailable integrations disclose missing configuration. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/bro-fq68bo)
- **Scalpal:** A mixed-reality surgical training environment synchronizes the headset, coach, dashboard, and simulated patient through SpacetimeDB. The database directly enables shared interactions. Its submission distinguishes simulation from measurements and says learning robot behavior from actual human practice remains untested; current training uses scripted demonstrations. [Submission](https://devpost.com/software/scalpal), [portfolio](https://mykm.dev/projects/scalpal)

## Gist: the best documented prompt-to-product mapping

The [official event page](https://law-di-gras.com/applied-ai-hackathon/) describes an October 2 competition around a real trial-law-firm workflow. The [briefing deck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1GEOsXDmPN4zX2WMtURYDGGV25f3pR9GX/view), with a [text transcription preserved in Gist’s repository](https://github.com/MatthewKim323/gist/blob/main/docs/research/deck.txt), supplies the specific challenge.

The brief asked for a visual dashboard that lets firm staff catch up on a case and gives lien-treating medical providers useful case visibility and communication. Users should not need to formulate the right question first. Live Clio access was required, writes to Clio were forbidden, hardcoded case content was disallowed, and submissions had to disclose models, cost, and unfinished work. The feature wish list was optional; teams could choose a focused implementation. The stated process included code review followed by a live pitch. [Preserved deck](https://github.com/MatthewKim323/gist/blob/main/docs/research/deck.txt)

Gist’s answer was a concise case digest, source-linked findings, contradictions, next actions, and a redacted provider view. Its portfolio describes comprehensive page processing, checking that quotations support claims, and caching unchanged inputs to control cost. This maps product choices to real adoption concerns: coverage, traceability, workflow, and recurring expense. Its claimed result is **top five**, not first place. [Project](https://mykm.dev/projects/gist)

The organizer’s results name Ross first, Corro second, and LAWGIQ third. A judge’s public reflection emphasizes who owns the workflow after the demo and who verifies material before an attorney uses it. That supports an operational-reliability interpretation, not an assumption that more agents or a larger graph wins. [Swans organizer feed](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/swans), [judge reflection](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/eleonorharutyunyan-businesscloudengineer_legaltech-appliedai-activity-7510726409229623297-AMCz)

## Rubrics that change how we should prepare

- **SB Hacks:** Completion, originality, track fit, learning, design, and technical complexity. Live demos were limited to 2.5 minutes plus 1.5 minutes of questions. Written submissions explicitly counted for sponsor judging. [Rules and rubric](https://sb-hacks-xii.devpost.com/)
- **FullyHacks:** Track alignment, execution, innovation, presentation, technical ability. [Rubric](https://fullyhacks.devpost.com/)
- **Broncohacks:** Impact, functionality, UX, technical complexity, presentation: 10 points each; creativity: 5. A complete, understandable product could gain more across the rubric than novelty alone. [Rubric](https://broncohacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- **HackDavis:** Social good, creativity, presentation, and track-specific criteria. [Rubric](https://hackdavis-2026.devpost.com/)
- **BeachHacks and Citrus:** Public rubric fields remained unfinished when checked. Do not invent precise weights. [BeachHacks](https://beachhacks-9-0.devpost.com/), [Citrus](https://citrus-hack-2026.devpost.com/)

## Application to our next build

There is direct evidence of the preparation process: Gist’s [research brief](https://github.com/MatthewKim323/gist/blob/main/docs/research/BRIEF.md) maps judges’ professional interests to proposed demo moments, distinguishes expected features from differentiators, schedules implementation, identifies features to cut, and scripts the pitch. These are the team’s planning hypotheses, not official scoring criteria. Its planned sequence moves from a provider’s status question to the firm dashboard, source evidence, provider sharing, and operating cost. This is stronger evidence of a repeatable method than architecture size.

1. Identify the exact award we can credibly compete for and the evidence its judges will inspect.
2. Name one user, one difficult moment, and one observable result. Write the demo sentence before the feature list.
3. Make the sponsor’s contribution visible in that result. Removing it should materially weaken the demonstration.
4. Prove one complete path with actual input, useful output, and a way to inspect what happened.
5. Give context an interface: source links, changed state, synchronized participants, or an action trace.
6. Make implementation claims precise. Label fixtures, simulation, cached examples, measured results, and future work honestly.
7. Use team strengths and accumulated knowledge to reduce build risk, while following the target event’s rules on prior code.
8. Prepare both the live demo and the written sponsor case; allocate rehearsal time within the published presentation limit.

Suggested review question: Can a judge see the problem, the useful change, the sponsor’s role, and the evidence it worked within the first minute?

No live application testing or judge scorecards were used. Technical descriptions are based on project submissions and portfolios; event requirements and award labels were cross-checked where public evidence was available.
