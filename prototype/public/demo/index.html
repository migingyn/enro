How it works

The appointment moves.
Your plan moves with it.

Enro reads the event time and relevant email updates, then works backward through travel, an arrival buffer, and time to get ready. When it is time to act, your phone rings with a reason to get going.

Phone signals give the call context. Picking up the phone means it moved. Fresh step counts, walking activity, and a spoken check in provide more evidence that you are getting moving. Location, when enabled, can help check whether the phone has left the starting area.

A real phone trial exposed a mistake: seven shakes registered as seven steps. The revised rule also checks Activity, but still needs physical validation. This film illustrates that rule. It is not a recording of a successful walking test.

Presentation inspired by Matthew Kim's Gist. One story, with the evidence behind each decision.

Read the film transcript
  1. A calendar time becomes a plan. A sample Price Center appointment starts at 6:30 PM. An email moves it to 6:20 PM.
  2. Enro works backward. With 20 minutes of travel, a 10 minute arrival buffer, and 45 minutes to get ready, preparation moves to 5:05 PM and departure to 5:50 PM.
  3. The phone rings with context. At departure time, the phone is still and no departure is confirmed. Enro says the appointment moved and it is time to head out.
  4. A pickup is not proof of walking. Seven counter increments and “I'm up” do not pass the revised rule when Activity says stationary.
  5. The response follows the evidence. In a second scripted scene, eight fresh counts, walking Activity, and a spoken check in pass the experimental movement rule.
  6. Leaving is a separate check. An optional location signal puts the phone outside the starting area. The route and arrival remain unconfirmed.
