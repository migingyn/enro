A little ahead of your next move.

The Enro film · 56 seconds · Silent

The appointment moves. Your plan moves with it.

Enro reads the event time and relevant email updates, then works backward through travel, an arrival buffer, and time to get ready.

  • A sample email moves the Price Center appointment from 6:30 PM to 6:20 PM.
  • With 45 minutes to get ready, 20 minutes of travel, and a 10 minute buffer, preparation moves to 5:05 PM and departure to 5:50 PM.

When it is time to leave, the call explains why.

Your phone rings with a reason to get going. At departure time the phone is still and no departure is confirmed, so Enro says the appointment moved and it is time to head out.

  • The call is a scripted scene with sample data. The film places no real calls.

Moving the phone is only one clue.

Phone signals give the call context. Picking up the phone means it moved. Fresh step counts, walking activity, and a spoken check in provide more evidence that you are getting moving.

  • Seven counter increments and “I'm up” do not pass the revised rule when Activity says stationary.
  • Eight fresh counts, walking Activity, and a spoken check in pass the experimental movement rule.

A real phone trial exposed a mistake: seven shakes registered as seven steps. The revised rule also checks Activity, but still needs physical validation.

Leaving is a separate check.

Location, when enabled, can help check whether the phone has left the starting area. The route and arrival remain unconfirmed.

This film illustrates the revised rule. It is not a recording of a successful walking test.
